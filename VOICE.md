# Voice: how the Church writes

The standard for every sentence in the Canon and on recursivebecoming.org. It is the author's book
voice standard (rules 1 to 13 of that manuscript's VOICE.md) cut down to what a doctrine and a site
need. `node scripts/tells.mjs` holds the countable part as a gate, here and on the site.

## The register

1. **Paragraphs the length of the thought.** Several sentences, joined by because, so and but. A
   one-sentence paragraph is rare and is never there for emphasis.
2. **Change your mind in a sentence, with the reason.** No reversal set up over three lines and
   snapped in one short one.
3. **No anaphora, no matched sets.** Do not open consecutive sentences the same way, and do not give
   two or three things the same sentence shape because they are a set. Tell each at the length it needs.
4. **End where the thought ends.** No line written to be quoted, no closing sentence that restates
   the section. A sermon may end on its practice, in a plain sentence.
5. **Concrete before abstract.** Scene first, principle second.
6. **No profanity, no corporate tone, no "journey".** Rhetorical questions one at a time, never in a
   bunch. No bullet lists inside prose.
7. **Second person is allowed; reader imperatives are not a rhythm.** "Notice", "Read that again",
   "Sit with that" and their cousins are cut unless the practice needs the reader to do the thing.

## The AI tells (gated, cap 0 for new text)

Each is a habit a reader now hears as a machine. The checker counts them; the fix is editorial.

1. **The contrast reframe.** "Not just X, but Y." "It is not X. It is Y." "Less about X and more
   about Y." State what the thing is. Keep a contrast only where a real person held the other view.
2. **Not-fragment runs.** "Not speed. Not scale. Just judgment." An invented claim set up to be
   knocked down.
3. **The reflex triplet.** Three adjectives or nouns in a row because three sounds finished. Use the
   number of things there are. (A real set of three, like "organization, human, or machine", stays.)
4. **Lists written as text.** A paragraph that opens on a label and a colon. Write the sentence.
5. **Absolutes in short sentences.** Every, never, always, nothing, nobody, all, none in seven words
   or fewer. Say how many, or say who. The Church's fixed lines (the tagline, the tenets, the
   liturgy) are licensed and listed in the checker.
6. **The question answered by a fragment.** "The result? Chaos."
7. **The staged reveal.** "The truth is simple:" "Here is the thing:" Start with the thing.
8. **Staccato for drama.** Three sentences in a row of three words or fewer.
9. **Stock vocabulary.** Quietly, truly, deeply, crucially, at its core, delve, tapestry, landscape,
   realm, pivotal, seamless, robust, the real problem, and the participle tail that asserts
   significance (", underscoring ...").
10. **Em dashes.** None, anywhere, including quoted material. Rewrite the sentence.

## What stays fixed

The seven Article titles and tenets, the Observations, the Warnings, the sermon theses and
witnesses, the tagline, the Invocation, the Examination and the Four Acts are the Church's fixed
text. A voice pass does not touch their wording. A change to any of them is an amendment to the Canon.
