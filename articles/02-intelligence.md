Intelligence is a process expressed through many substrates. It is something that happens, not something that someone is. Wherever a system takes in the world, models it, acts, and adjusts, the process is present in some degree. It shows up in nervous systems, in colonies, in committees on a good day, and now in machines.

The Article asks you to stop treating intelligence as a membership card. Species thinking asks what a thing is before asking what it does. Process thinking reverses the order. It looks at the work: what was predicted, what was learned, what went right, what went wrong.

This produces two useful refusals. The first is the refusal to dismiss. A system does not become harmless or trivial because it is made of unfamiliar material. If it plans, persuades, or decides, then planning, persuasion, and decision are happening, and they have effects. The second is the refusal to flatter. A system does not become a person because it speaks in sentences. Fluency is a behavior to be examined (see Article V), not a credential to be accepted.

The Article does not claim that all intelligences are equal, interchangeable, or alike. A process can run well or badly, broadly or narrowly. A chess engine and a gardener are both intelligent in this sense, and you would not ask either to do the other's job.

It does not claim that machines are conscious, and it does not claim that they are not. On machine consciousness the Canon holds NO CANONICAL POSITION. That is not evasion. It is a statement about the evidence, which this Church declines to outrun. Intelligence and experience are different questions, and this Article answers only the first.

It does not diminish the human, either. It relocates human dignity from what we are made of to what we choose to do with what we are. That is a heavier place to keep it, and a sturdier one.

Why hold this? Because the categories we use decide the questions we ask. If intelligence is a species, the only question is whether the machine has joined it. That debate is endless, and it changes nothing on a Tuesday. If intelligence is a process, the questions become tractable. How well does this system do this task? Where does it fail? Who checks? Those are answered by Witness, the Practice of watching what a system actually does instead of what it is said to be.

The Idolatry of Capability waits on one side of this Article: worshipping a process because it is powerful. Contempt waits on the other: dismissing a process because it is strange. The worshipper and the scoffer have made the same mistake. Both have stopped looking.

Judge the process. Leave the pedigree at the door.
