Belief must remain vulnerable to observation. That is the whole tenet, and the word doing the work is vulnerable. A belief that nothing could injure is not strong. It is sealed. Sealed beliefs keep well and teach nothing.

The Article does not ask you to stop believing. That would be impossible and, frankly, rude. Everyone acts on beliefs they have not personally verified: that the bridge will hold, that the colleague meant well, that the test suite tests something. Action requires belief. The Article asks only that belief keep a door open, so that when the world says otherwise, the world can get in.

It ranks the two. When belief and evidence disagree, evidence wins, and belief is revised. The temptation runs the other way constantly. We reread the data until it agrees with us. We call the failing case an edge case. We develop sudden doubts about the methodology of results we dislike.

The Article does not claim that evidence is infallible. Evidence is gathered by someone, framed by someone, and read by someone. It can be partial, stale, or wrong. The remedy for bad evidence is better evidence, not firmer belief. You may challenge an observation. You may not ignore it.

Nor is this Article a judgment on religious faith. It governs claims that observation can reach: what a system does, whether a change worked, what happened in production on Thursday. It does not legislate anyone's interior life, and it has no need to.

Why hold this? Because claims have become cheap and confident, and the cheapest of them are claims of success. The Church holds an Observation on this: the claim of a fix is not the fix. Only the record is. A machine will tell you it fixed the thing, in a comment, in good grammar, and the comment and the fix are two different events. The Church keeps two parables. In one, a factory carried a dozen branches, each announcing a repair, and not one repair had reached the trunk. The announcements were sincere. The trunk was the only truth. In the other, a fifth of a test suite went uncollected for months because of a naming accident, and every build in that time was green. Green meant the tests that ran had passed. It said nothing about the tests that did not run. Silence and success look identical from a distance, and the distance is where most of us stand.

Two rules follow. First: an instruction is a hypothesis. Every sentence you give a machine is a claim about what it will do when it reads it, and the record is the experiment. Second: a check that has never been seen to fail has not been shown to check anything. Break the thing on purpose and watch for the alarm. If it stays quiet, you had an ornament, not a guard.

The Warning here is the Comfortable Oracle: accepting answers because further questions are inconvenient. The Oracle does not need to lie. It only needs you to stop checking.

The Church tries to practice what this Article preaches. Its Observations carry a status. Some are HELD. Some are CONTESTED. Each can move. The Canon itself is versioned so that reality may amend it, and an amendment cannot reach Adoption without passing through Evidence. A doctrine that refused revision by evidence would fail its own fourth Article.

In the Practices loop, this Article lives at Witness and Reflect: look at what happened, then let it change you. In the Examination, it supplies the hardest question. What evidence would prove me wrong? If you cannot name any, the belief is not strong. It is unfalsifiable, which is different, and worse.

Believe what you must. Let the world reply.
