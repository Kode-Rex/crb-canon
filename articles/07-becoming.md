No doctrine, system, organization, human, or machine is complete. The tenet's list is long on purpose, because it has to cover you, this Canon, whatever you are building this week, and whatever is helping you build it.

This is the Article that gives the Church its name. Becoming is the condition of anything that is still learning, and recursive becoming is what happens when the learning feeds back into the learner, so that a system which improves systems ends up improving itself and the Canon amends itself. That is why the Practices are drawn as a loop, Query, Build, Witness, Reflect, Revise and then Query again, with no step on it marked Done.

But a loop has to climb or it is only a wheel. A pass that counts comes back with something the last pass did not have, maybe a better question or a limit the old run would have let through, and a pass that comes back to the same place with the same tools is a retry. A retry is activity rather than becoming, and a system can be very busy without having moved.

Becoming is also safe only inside something that does not become along with it. The Church holds that what is cheap to remake will be remade, and that this is fine as long as the walls hold. The walls are the objective, the limits, the standard of evidence, and the authority to change any of those. Whatever sits inside them may be fluid, but the walls themselves have to be solid, because a loop with soft walls leaks instead of climbing. When a thing has been replaced, the old one goes, because two versions kept side by side just in case belong to a system that has not decided what it is, and such a system piles up instead of becoming.

None of this means that nothing is worth committing to. A bridge will need maintenance for as long as it stands, and people drive across it every day, because provisional and worthless are different things. The Canon stands at v1.0.0 and the Church holds it seriously, in the way a carpenter holds a tool rather than a trophy, which is to say it gets used, and replaced when a better one turns up.

It also does not claim that every change is an improvement, because becoming can go badly. A system drifts, an organization forgets why it did things, and a person grows in a direction they regret later, so incompleteness is only an opening to improve and carries no guarantee that the improvement happens. That is why revision runs through evidence rather than enthusiasm, and why the Church prefers a floor that only rises, meaning a standard that catches its own violations, repairs them, and then raises the bar by the height of the repair.

And it does not forbid finishing things. Releases still ship and tickets still close, because finishing a piece of work is a necessary act and the Church has no quarrel with it. The error is in treating the finished piece as the end of the work.

The Church holds this because the claim of completeness is the most comfortable claim on offer. Once a thing is declared finished, the questions stop, so the doctrine can stop listening and the organization can stop asking whether its reasons still hold, and nobody schedules a check on a system that is done. Each of the Warnings in the Canon gets easier to fall into at that point, and the Comfortable Oracle most of all, since it lives on the belief that the answer has already been given.

The Observation that systems that improve systems change who does what is HELD, and if that holds then no arrangement of people and machines around a piece of work is final either. The roles you hold today were assigned by conditions that are already moving.

So the Church builds its own incompleteness into its structure. The Canon is versioned like software, and an amendment passes through Proposal, Witness, Debate, Evidence, and Adoption before the version bumps. Scripture is usually handed down as finished truth, but this Canon stays unfinished so that reality can amend it, and the Articles are held up in the light where anyone can examine them and correct them when the evidence requires it.

That includes this Article. If evidence ever showed that something had become complete, the Church would have to revise its seventh Article, and it would want to see that evidence. Until it arrives, the Church counts not being finished as good news, because work that is unfinished can still get better.
